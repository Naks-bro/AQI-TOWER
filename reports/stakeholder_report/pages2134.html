Water-Spray Stage — Phase 18 Decision
The original concept included a water-spray stage. Phase 18 reviewed it rigorously. The result was NO-GO.
Phase 18 was a research-only engineering feasibility assessment — no CFD, no physical experiment. The analysis reviewed aerosol physics, pressure-drop impact, biological risk standards, water handling complexity, and alternatives.
The core physics problem: simple low-energy spray towers are ineffective for PM2.5 (0.1–2 µm). This is the "Greenfield gap" — the particle size range where neither inertial impaction nor diffusion produces useful collection in a compact chamber.
Why the water stage was rejected
PM2.5 irrelevant: Greenfield gap — simple spray cannot capture 0.3–2.5 µm particles at this scale
Coarse dust better served by dry G4/M5 pre-filter — same function, no water, no risk
Hygiene risk is disqualifying: Legionella and biofilm in recirculating warm-water spray (ASHRAE 188, CIBSE TM13)
Carryover threatens HEPA: droplets reaching filter face cause wetting and microbial loading
35–125 Pa added ΔP — reduces airflow 7–23% from an already-constrained operating point
12+ extra components — pump, tank, nozzles, demister — for no unique benefit
Replacement: G4/M5 washable pre-filter
Achieves coarse pre-collection upstream of HEPA. No water, no pump, no Legionella risk. One component. ~25–50 Pa clean ΔP. Washable monthly. Widely available in India at low cost.
New treatment sequence: Inlet → [G4/M5 washable pre-filter] → [Activated carbon bed] → [H13 HEPA] → Clean riser → KVO 250 fan → Outlet

Eight Lessons from the Engineering Process
01
A good-looking airflow path is not necessarily a good airflow path.
V00 had plausible visual flow but significant recirculation and pressure losses. Three geometry iterations were needed to reach a well-behaved field.
02
A fan must be evaluated with system resistance.
Adding real fan curve data (V01) fundamentally changed the simulation. A fan delivers the flow at which system resistance equals fan output — not a fixed number.
03
Filters create large pressure penalties that shift the operating point.
The H13 HEPA added ~111 Pa — reducing airflow from ~1,496 to ~1,332 m³/h. Every treatment stage competes for the same fan headroom.
04
Bypass paths matter as much as filter efficiency.
45% bypass — discovered in simulation — showed that a highly rated filter can be functionally irrelevant if air routes around it. Finding this early was the value of the simulation.
05
Particle trajectories are not filtration efficiency.
62% of simulated particles reaching the filter face is a geometry-routing result, not a removal efficiency. No capture probability was applied because it is not yet available.
06
Gas removal is pollutant-specific and requires pollutant-specific evidence.
A documented comparison of six gas species was needed before HCHO could be selected. Generic "carbon removes VOCs" is not an engineering claim.
07
Missing data should lead to an experiment — not an invented coefficient.
Gas-phase CFD was blocked, correctly. The packed-bed experiment exists specifically to generate the inputs that will make CFD meaningful.
08
A stage that looks useful can be eliminated by physics.
The water-spray stage was part of the original concept. Phase 18 showed that basic aerosol physics makes it ineffective for PM2.5 while introducing serious hygiene risks. Removing it was correct.

Claims We Do Not Make
Responsible engineering means being equally clear about what you do not know.
No validated CADR
Clean Air Delivery Rate requires physical measurement under standard conditions. No CADR value has been established for the AQI Tower at any stage of development.
No PM2.5 removal efficiency
The H13 classification is a component rating at test conditions. Without η(d_p) at operating velocity, no system-level PM2.5 efficiency can be calculated from available data.
No HCHO removal efficiency
No breakthrough experiment has been performed. The carbon stage has no measured capacity, no breakthrough time, and no gas-phase CADR.
No claim of physical bypass = 0%
CFD bypass ≈ 0% is a modelled result. Whether a fabricated tower is physically airtight depends entirely on manufacturing quality — not CFD.
62% is not particle removal
62% is a simulated particle arrival fraction at the filter plane. Filter capture efficiency η(d_p) has not been applied — it is not available for this product.
1,332 m³/h is not measured airflow
1,332 m³/h is a RANS CFD result from a partially converged case. It is a useful screening estimate. Real tower airflow has never been measured.
