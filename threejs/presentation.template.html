How long for a room?

ASSUMED SCENARIO — not measured prototype performance. Bigger rooms need more clean air or more time. This estimates a reduction in airborne particles, not when a room becomes safe.

Clean-air rate (CADR) counts particle-cleaned air, not just fan airflow. D01's actual CADR is UNKNOWN. The default 150 is an example, not its rating; different particle sizes can have different rates.

—

Ideal model: well-mixed enclosed room, fixed clean-air rate, no new particles or outdoor influx; natural settling and ventilation excluded. No guarantee for occupied/leaky rooms or outdoors. The displayed person is only a scale marker, not an occupant generating pollution.

Technical formula and sources

C(t)/C(0) = exp(−CADR × t / (60 × volume)). Volume = area × height. Time = −60 × volume × ln(1−reduction) / CADR. These are particle mass-balance scenarios, not CFD or disease-risk estimates.

Sources: EPA: CADR and room sizing; CDC: ideal clearance equation and mixing limits. Checked 5 October 2026. We adapt the equation to effective particle clean-air delivery; no medical clearance or certification is claimed.

What it targets

First prototype: airborne particles such as dust and fine smoke particles (PM2.5/PM10). Actual capture and room reduction must be measured. Smoke gases are not included.

Not established: CO₂, carbon monoxide, VOCs/odours, bacteria/virus control or health protection. No gas-adsorption, water or solar stage is fitted to this prototype. Filtration is not a replacement for ventilation or removing pollution sources.
